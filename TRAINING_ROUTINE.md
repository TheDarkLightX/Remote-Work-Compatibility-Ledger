# Remote Work Training Routine

This repository is a standing capability-maintenance program for a labor market being reshaped by AI.

The objective is not to collect courses, credentials, or toy projects. It is to maintain and expand the ability to perform valuable remote knowledge work, with and without AI, while continuously converting that ability into inspectable evidence.

## One active goal at a time

There is exactly **one active career-training project**.

A new calendar week does **not** automatically create a new project. If the current project is incomplete, it simply remains active and the next weekly brief identifies the highest-value remaining tasks. Missing a week is treated like missing a workout: resume the program rather than restarting it or creating guilt-driven backlog.

The active goal lives in [`CURRENT.md`](CURRENT.md).

## Weekly career-trainer loop

At the weekly check-in:

1. **Inspect the current project.** Determine what evidence actually exists and what remains incomplete.
2. **Carry forward if necessary.** If the definition of done has not been met, keep the same project and reprioritize the remaining work.
3. **Graduate completed work.** Score it conservatively, replay it where possible, record AI use, and promote only defensible capability claims.
4. **Research the market only when a new project is needed.** Extract recurring task primitives from legitimate remote jobs, contracts, AI-evaluation/QA work, and permission-light opportunities.
5. **Choose one next project.** Optimize for employability signal per unit of human attention and AI/Codex usage.
6. **Write the brief.** State the goal, why it matters, task checklist, definition of done, what AI should handle, what the candidate must personally understand, and what evidence the project unlocks.

No daily schedule is required. Work can be done whenever energy and other responsibilities permit.

## Project selection objective

Choose projects that maximize approximately:

```text
Career Value =
  current demand
  × breadth across role families
  × fit with existing strengths
  × AI leverage
  × verifiability
  × reviewer signal-to-noise
  × durability of skill
  ÷ human attention cost
```

Prefer compact projects that prove several valuable task primitives at once.

Reject projects that are primarily:

- tutorial consumption;
- resume decoration;
- generic CRUD demos;
- duplicated by stronger existing evidence;
- large for the sake of being large;
- difficult to explain or replay;
- tied to a fading task unless useful for immediate bridge income.

A typical project should be bounded enough to finish without taking over the rest of life. The previous 6–10 hour estimate is a guideline, not a deadline.

## Evidence modes

- **U — Unaided:** independent work, candidate-attested unless externally proctored.
- **A — AI-augmented:** AI/Codex permitted and disclosed; the human remains responsible for correctness and claim scope.
- **V — AI verification/supervision:** evaluate, challenge, repair, or reject AI-produced work.

The long-run objective is not to maximize U-mode. Modern knowledge work often rewards intelligent tool use. The objective is to retain enough unaided competence to understand and defend the work while becoming unusually strong in A- and V-mode.

## Human ownership rule

AI may do large amounts of mechanical work when a benchmark allows it, but each project must contain a human-owned core.

The candidate must personally be able to:

- explain the objective and success criteria;
- identify what could make the result misleading;
- inspect important edge cases;
- defend the principal design/analysis choices;
- reproduce or reason through a representative subset without relying on hidden AI work;
- state the strongest claim the evidence supports and what it does **not** support.

## Verification before promotion

A project is not portfolio evidence merely because code exists.

Before promotion, check as applicable:

- required deliverables exist;
- tests and adversarial cases run;
- clean replay succeeds;
- data and IDs line up;
- the scoring method itself has been challenged;
- AI use/provenance is disclosed accurately;
- limitations and failure cases are documented;
- a short live defense can be answered without AI assistance.

## Capability status vocabulary

Use conservative states:

- **planned** — benchmark selected but not started;
- **in progress** — work exists but definition of done is unmet;
- **completed** — required artifact exists;
- **replayed** — a clean run reproduced the committed result;
- **portfolio-ready** — score threshold met with no unresolved critical failure and the work can be defended;
- **externally verified** — an identified third party independently evaluated or proctored it.

Do not silently upgrade these states.

## Training priorities

Default priority order:

1. capabilities appearing repeatedly in paid remote work;
2. capabilities likely to remain valuable as AI improves;
3. verification, judgment, ownership, system integration, research, QA, and accountability;
4. skills demonstrable with compact replayable artifacts;
5. gaps blocking several plausible role families at once;
6. interview/live-defense competence around work already demonstrated.

## Portfolio admission rule

A training project does not automatically appear on the public-facing capabilities page.

Promote it only when it is:

- directly relevant to plausible paid work;
- understandable quickly;
- accurately disclosed;
- reproducible where applicable;
- adversarially tested;
- strong enough to survive questioning.

Weak or unfinished work can remain in training history without diluting the public signal.

## Long-run outcome

The ledger becomes a time series of capability rather than a static resume:

```text
market demand
    ↓
one active goal
    ↓
AI-augmented construction
    ↓
human judgment + verification
    ↓
replayable evidence
    ↓
live defense
    ↓
capability map
```

**Candidate claims. Work decides.**
